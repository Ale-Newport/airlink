Two phones. No signal.

AirLink connects two phones directly over Bluetooth LE and local Wi-Fi, with no internet, no account and no server. Chat, send photos and voice notes, play one of 28 two-player games, and watch a film in sync, end-to-end encrypted.

Run the real protocol, right here

This page bundles @airlink/core unchanged: the same TypeScript that performs the handshake, encryption, retransmission and transport migration on a phone. Two simulated devices talk over a simulated radio on a virtual clock, so a minute of Bluetooth takes milliseconds and every run is exactly repeatable.

Pick a scenario. Nothing here touches a network.

What it does

Chat

Text, photos, voice notes, replies, reactions, read receipts and typing, stored only on the device.

Play

28 two-player games, from chess with the full rules to real-time air hockey, sorted by what the link can carry.

Share

Chunked, resumable, verified file transfer with an honest ETA, even at Bluetooth speeds.

Watch together

Play the same local video on both phones, kept in step to within tens of milliseconds.

Private by construction

Ed25519 identities, a SIGMA-I handshake and ChaCha20-Poly1305, with no account and no server to leak.

Survives the real world

A session outlives its link: drop Bluetooth, reconnect over Wi-Fi, and not one message is lost.

The honest part

With no network, iPhone and Android share exactly one radio: Bluetooth Low Energy. AirLink is built for that slow path and upgrades invisibly when something faster appears.
